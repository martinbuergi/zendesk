MadeiraMadeira transforms CX with Zendesk AI agents

With an ecosystem connecting e-commerce, marketplaces, physical stores, and home services, MadeiraMadeira needed to scale its customer service operation without sacrificing quality in a journey that is highly emotional for customers. Using Zendesk Suite, AI agents, Copilot, WFM, QA, and Premier Support, the company advanced its automation strategy to solve real problems, give teams more autonomy, and prepare its operation to grow more efficiently—with the customer at the center of every decision.

Building experiences that become part of people’s lives

Some purchases mean much more than a transaction. They mark new chapters in people’s lives.

Founded in 2009, MadeiraMadeira became Brazil’s largest platform for furniture, décor, and home products. What began as an e-commerce business grew into a complete ecosystem spanning its website and app, marketplaces such as Mercado Livre, Shopee, and Amazon, and physical stores in different regions of Brazil.

The company also expanded into home-related services, including furniture assembly and solutions that support the customer throughout their journey.

For MadeiraMadeira, serving a customer means being part of important moments: a first home, a wedding, the arrival of a child, or a major life change. That made customer experience a strategic priority for the business.

As Alan Barsi, MadeiraMadeira’s Vice President of Operational Excellence and CX, explains: “When we provide excellent service, we become part of a dream and stay in the customer’s memory. If we fail, that frustration happens at a very sensitive moment, too.”

That responsibility grew as the company expanded. New sales channels, a broader product range, and increasingly complex logistics required a strategy that could maintain service quality without a proportional increase in operational resources.

In this context, the company began to see artificial intelligence not as an end in itself, but as a way to deliver better experiences at scale.

Growing without adding complexity

MadeiraMadeira’s expansion brought new challenges to its customer service operation.

Along with managing millions of products and multiple sales channels, the company had to handle a distinctive logistics operation. Unlike small items, furniture requires specialized transportation, in-home delivery, assembly, and often technical support for damaged or missing parts. Each interaction carried significant operational and emotional complexity.

Meanwhile, the business’s continued growth raised an important question: how could it keep serving more customers without relying on a matching increase in the customer service team?

According to Alan Barsi, this was one of the main reasons for transforming the operation. “As a fast-growing company, we needed to use technology to scale, solve customers’ problems better, and become much more efficient.”

Another challenge lay in the technology infrastructure itself. The company was still operating on a legacy system, with information about orders and logistics processes spread across different sources. Before expanding its use of AI, it needed to build a consistent, reliable data foundation capable of supporting automated decisions.

For Felipe Toazza, MadeiraMadeira’s Vice President of Technology, this work was critical to the project’s success. “When you think about automation, the problem usually isn’t the conversation. The challenge is having reliable data to answer the customer and robust solutions to resolve more complex problems.”

Instead of completing a full migration all at once, the company chose a gradual approach. The implementation took place in waves, with channel-by-channel rollouts, hypercare monitoring, and A/B testing to validate each stage before expanding across the operation. This approach reduced risk, accelerated team learning, and created a solid foundation for growing the use of automation over time.

Technology is most powerful when it improves people’s work

Any technology transformation requires more than a new platform. It depends on the trust of the people who will use it every day.

At MadeiraMadeira, the adoption of AI focused on solving real business problems and making teams’ work more meaningful. Rather than presenting automation as a replacement for human service, the company showed how technology could eliminate repetitive tasks, allowing specialists to focus on more complex work that brings greater value to customers.

“The truth is that people are naturally resistant to change. But when they start using the technology and see that it improves their work, they begin advocating for the transformation themselves,” says Barsi.

This shift was felt across different roles in the operation. Agents spent less time searching for information across multiple systems or repeating standard answers, and more time acting as specialists in resolving complex cases. Quality analysts began using AI to review large volumes of conversations, significantly increasing their ability to identify opportunities for improvement.

The impact was immediate in the technology team, too. According to Toazza, the team quickly saw the initiative’s value when they noticed its results reflected directly in the customer experience. “For a developer, seeing that what they implemented is bringing automation, customer satisfaction, and improvements to people’s lives is extremely motivating. It creates a very positive cycle within the team.”

The transformation was supported by adopting Zendesk Suite as the central platform for customer service, combined with Zendesk AI agents, Copilot, WFM, and QA. Implementation also involved reviewing the company’s data architecture and consolidating single sources of information for orders and logistics—an essential step in ensuring that automated responses to customers were reliable.

With this foundation in place, MadeiraMadeira began continuously improving its customer service workflows. New automations were first made available to a portion of customers, then monitored through A/B testing and hypercare, and adjusted before being expanded across the operation. This process enabled faster innovation without compromising the customer experience.

Results that transformed the customer experience

For MadeiraMadeira, success with AI is not measured by the number of automations implemented, but by how quickly and effectively problems are resolved.

This view led the company to challenge a common assumption in customer service: that consumers always prefer to speak with a person. “In reality, the customer wants to resolve their problem as quickly and effectively as possible. They don’t mind talking to an AI agent. The problem is when the agent doesn’t understand or can’t solve what they need,” explains Alan Barsi.

MadeiraMadeira uses AI agents to resolve cases where automation adds real value, while more complex situations continue to be routed to human specialists. This helps the company avoid repetitive journeys that don’t lead to a resolution and keep the focus on the customer experience throughout the interaction.

The results of this approach began to appear quickly. According to the company, customer satisfaction among people served by the AI agent became higher than for interactions handled by human agents. The platform also helped the company absorb significant increases in demand during seasonal periods such as Black Friday without compromising service quality.

Another important gain was greater autonomy for business teams. Previously, any change to customer service workflows depended directly on the technology team. As the platform evolved, different teams began creating and adjusting customer journeys independently, speeding up improvements for customers and reducing internal bottlenecks.

This progress also opened the door to new kinds of automation. One example shared by the company involves damaged furniture. In some situations, the system can assess the customer’s problem and automatically issue a payment via Pix to provide an immediate solution—reducing a process that used to take much longer to around four minutes.

At the same time, the operation began to grow more sustainably. Instead of increasing customer service resources in proportion to the business’s expansion, MadeiraMadeira began using AI to handle repetitive tasks, giving specialists more time for cases that require knowledge, empathy, and human judgment.

Anticipating needs to transform customer service

For MadeiraMadeira, AI represents a much bigger change than automating responses. The next step is to build experiences that resolve problems from end to end, reducing customer effort and making service increasingly proactive.

In Felipe Toazza’s view, the market is still exploring only part of AI’s potential. Many automated solutions work like an updated version of traditional FAQs: they answer questions or look up information. The real difference, though, comes from creating agents that can take action and deliver complete solutions for customers.

“Today, there’s a move toward agentic solutions, but it’s still in its early stages. The future lies in resolving a problem from end to end—whether that means rescheduling a delivery or an assembly appointment, or taking other actions that truly address the customer’s needs.”

That vision is already beginning to take shape at MadeiraMadeira. In some situations, automated agents can identify a problem, assess the context, and offer a practical solution in just a few minutes. For the company, this shows how AI can go beyond conversation and take an active role in resolving customer requests.

At the same time, the company believes technology should be used to rethink processes, not simply digitize old ways of working.

“Using these capabilities just to make a bad process faster doesn’t solve the problem. This is a moment to redesign processes and understand how we can address the customer’s pain in a completely different way,” says Alan Barsi.

This shift in perspective also expands the role of customer experience within the organization. With thousands of conversations taking place every day, AI can identify trends, consolidate lessons, and uncover opportunities that would be impossible to analyze manually. This information helps the company better understand its customers and guide improvements beyond the customer service operation.

The goal is to create an increasingly proactive experience, where the company can anticipate needs before they turn into support requests. This vision guides the operation’s continued development and reinforces technology’s role in supporting business strategy.

As Felipe Toazza puts it: “Technology is a means to grow the business and deliver solutions. In customer service, it exists to make the customer journey better and help create an experience that makes customers want to come back.”

With Zendesk’s support, MadeiraMadeira continues to evolve its customer service strategy to keep pace with the business’s growth and the complexity of its operation. Beyond adopting new technologies, the company is building an operation designed to scale intelligently, strengthen its teams’ autonomy, and offer customers experiences that are increasingly simple, fast, and relevant.
