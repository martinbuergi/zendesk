Mimecast transforms customer support with Zendesk AI

Mimecast is transforming customer support into a competitive advantage with Zendesk AI agents. By automating common requests, accelerating resolutions, and routing customers to the right engineers with the right context, the cybersecurity company is delivering faster service, empowering support teams to tackle more complex issues, and creating a stronger customer experience.

For cybersecurity company Mimecast, customer support has become more than a reactive function. It’s now one of the company’s strongest competitive advantages.

“Support is a selling point,” says Will O’Keeffe, Senior Vice President of Customer Experience and Delivery at Mimecast. “It’s a key service differentiator for Mimecast.”

Using Zendesk AI agents, Mimecast is helping customers get faster answers while enabling engineers to move straight to resolution.

“If AI can resolve an issue, great,” O’Keeffe says. “If it can’t, we’ve already captured the customer’s intent and routed the case to the right engineers.”

Early beta testing with Zendesk AI agents has demonstrated as much as 70 percent deflection in some product categories.

Shifting engineers to higher-value work

Prior to Zendesk, Mimecast was using Salesforce Service Cloud as their support platform, but it was viewed as too complex, too developer-dependent, and too slow for the company’s business needs.

In June 2024, Mimecast selected Zendesk to replace Salesforce, as it offered faster change management, easier administration, lower complexity, and a better cost-to-effort ratio for the company’s internal and external service and support workflows.

It launched a highly customized implementation with a complex integration architecture. Despite the technical complexity, the migration was successfully completed with the help of Zendesk’s Professional Services team in a phased approach, delivering a modern support platform and early value.

When O’Keeffe first took over the organization, support was often viewed as a weakness. Today, support has become one of Mimecast’s strongest retention tools.

“We have customers who are staying simply because of support,” he says. “They’ve evaluated competitors, but they know that if there’s a problem—we’ve got their back.”

That transformation matters because Mimecast protects organizations from email, human, and AI-driven threats, meaning customers often contact support during urgent, business-critical security incidents.

“What customers need to know very quickly is that we understand their issue, we’re able to help them immediately, and we’re able to contain any potential problem,” O’Keeffe says.

As Zendesk AI agents handle routine support requests, Mimecast’s engineers spend more time solving complex problems and acting as trusted advisors. AI now automates much of that investigative work before engineers ever get involved, allowing them to focus on solving the customer’s actual problem rather than performing repetitive analysis.

“There are cases where customers think something should be easy, but historically there was a lot of manual engineering work happening behind the scenes,” O’Keeffe says. “AI gives us an easy button.”

Mimecast also uses AI to analyze videos and screenshots, turning complex technical issues into actionable problem statements and helping engineers resolve them more quickly.

Rebuilding customer support from the ground up

When O’Keeffe joined Mimecast, he inherited a support organization that relied on an outdated tiered model and fragmented processes.

“It was operating like IBM in 1991,” he says.

Support engineers were expected to know seven to nine different products while handling almost every interaction by phone. Cases routinely bounced from tier to tier, creating delays and unnecessary complexity.

“We had very little visibility into what was coming in, how quickly we were responding, or how quickly we were actually solving problems,” O’Keeffe says.

More importantly, the organization lacked a shared sense of ownership.

“Managers were comfortable operating with an old mindset: ‘If it’s not my job, it’s not my problem,’” he says. “We need everyone to understand that every case is your problem—from the moment you see it until it’s resolved.”

That cultural shift proved just as important as the technology itself.

“The technology was a six-month problem,” O’Keeffe says. “The people were an 18-month problem.”

Putting customers—not metrics—at the center

Instead of optimizing around internal operational metrics, Mimecast rebuilt its support organization around what customers actually value.

“Customers don’t care how many cases you get,” O’Keeffe says. “They only care about their case.”

That customer-first approach has helped Mimecast reduce response times dramatically, going from a massive backlog to answering almost all digital inquiries within an hour.

Instead of expecting every engineer to master every product, Mimecast organized support into specialized pods where experienced engineers coach teammates while building deeper product expertise.

“The customer doesn’t care if they get the same engineer,” O’Keeffe says. “They care if they get their problem resolved quickly.”

Support as a growth engine

The transformation at Mimecast has fundamentally changed how customers—and the company itself—view support. Looking ahead, Mimecast sees AI as a force multiplier, not a replacement for people.

“The dirty secret about AI is that it still requires people,” O’Keeffe says. “You need good people who understand the customer experience, who understand the problems you’re trying to solve, and who continually improve the workflows and outcomes.”

For O’Keeffe, that’s what separates successful AI implementations from unsuccessful ones.

“Don’t think of AI as something that simply clears away the noise,” he says. “Invest in it properly, manage it continuously, and use it to solve meaningful customer problems. That’s where the real value comes from.”
