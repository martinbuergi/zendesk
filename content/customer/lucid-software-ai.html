Lucid Software scales support for 100 million users with Zendesk AI

Lucid Software scaled support for more than 100 million users by building a strong self-service foundation and then investing in Zendesk AI agents. By automating increasingly complex support scenarios, Lucid improved accuracy, increased ticket deflection, reduced support demand, and freed teams to focus on higher-value work and innovation.

Lucid Software helps teams turn ideas into action. With products like Lucidchart for intelligent diagramming, Lucidspark for virtual whiteboarding and airfocus by Lucid for product intelligence, organizations can visualize workflows, align teams around shared context, and accelerate execution.

But as Lucid’s customer base expanded from nine million users in 2016 to more than 100 million users today, the company faced a challenge familiar to many fast-growing technology businesses—how to deliver exceptional support without dramatically increasing support volume.
“The biggest challenge we faced was around scale,” says Keyvan Sadigh, VP of Customer Operations at Lucid Software. “We were using Gmail to tackle all of our customer emails that were coming in—and often repeating ourselves in our responses.”

Beyond the limitations of a shared inbox, Lucid lacked the self-service resources customers needed to find answers on their own. To address this, Sadigh and his team turned to Zendesk AI for the next stage of their scaling strategy.

Today, Lucid uses Zendesk across the business—from customer support and self-service resources to employee service for HR and IT.

Solving problems quickly with AI

After exhausting traditional approaches to scaling support, Lucid began evaluating how Zendesk AI could help the company go further.

“We have tried to pilot AI since 2019 with different vendors,” says Sadigh. “But with Zendesk AI agents, we felt like, okay—this is something that we can really invest in.”

Lucid’s use of AI builds on a support philosophy that the company has refined for years—helping customers solve problems as quickly as possible.

“For about 90 to 95 percent of Lucid customers, they actually prefer the self-service model,” says Sadigh. “When they have a question, they often want to figure out how to solve it themselves.”

Lucid deployed Zendesk AI agents to recognize approximately 20 common support issues.

“Once it tags tickets in one of the categories we’ve identified, it will then send the customer an immediate response that’s been pre-written by our support team,” Sadigh says. “That really addresses the crux of what the user is facing—and allows them to self-help their own solution.”

While Lucid has seen impressive results, its confidence in AI didn’t happen overnight. Early pilots averaged between 50 and 60 percent accuracy. After only a few weeks of optimization, Lucid increased accuracy for these pilots to more than 90 percent.

“When you’re going into the AI space, you’re not just betting on the present—you’re betting on the future,” says Sadigh.

Deflecting thousands of tickets without sacrificing service

That preference for self-service ultimately laid the foundation for Lucid’s AI strategy.

“The key metric that we track for our support operations is what we call the escalation rate,” says Sadigh. “Relative to our user base, what is the volume of tickets being handled by human agents?”

In its first year with Zendesk AI, Lucid reduced support demand to just 0.12 tickets per 10,000 active users over a quarterly period—an extraordinarily low figure for a software platform serving more than 100 million users.

“We’ve really been able to lower the number of tickets per user base,” says Sadigh. “Additionally, we are looking at deflection—the number of tickets that Zendesk AI is able to handle with no human agent involvement, what we call zero-touch support.”

The results are especially notable because Lucid had already optimized many of the traditional ways to scale support, including self-service content, community forums, and automated responses.

By the time it introduced AI agents, the company was focused on automating increasingly complex support scenarios rather than simple, repetitive requests. Lucid set a goal of achieving 5,000 ticket deflections in its first year.

“Five thousand deflections doesn’t sound like a lot in isolation,” says Sadigh. “But when you know everything that we’ve already done to get to where we are, 5,000 deflections from start to finish would have a very meaningful impact on our business.”

Creating new opportunities for support teams

As AI agents take on repetitive work, Lucid isn’t shrinking its support organization. Instead, it’s redefining what support professionals do.

“The real power of AI is it handles what in the past was known as tier-one support, which then allows my team to really focus on the more technical challenges, the relationships with product and engineering, and even the data analysis side of things,” says Sadigh.

By automating routine requests, Lucid’s support team can spend more time identifying customer pain points, influencing product decisions, and building the next generation of AI-powered experiences.

Betting on the future together

As Lucid continues expanding its AI strategy, the company views Zendesk as a long-term ally in shaping the future of customer service.

“We really wanted to go with a partner that we were all in with,” says Sadigh. “Zendesk has been that for us.”

Lucid envisions a future where AI agents communicate directly with one another, resolving issues across products and platforms without requiring customers to navigate multiple support channels.

Until then, the company remains focused on a simple goal—helping customers solve problems faster than ever before.
