DFIN unifies service and drives 80% first-call resolution with Zendesk

Donnelley Financial Solutions (DFIN) transformed fragmented support into a unified service model with Zendesk. By improving employee experience, gaining real-time data insights, and testing innovations internally, DFIN increased efficiency and first-call resolution. Now, with AI, the company is scaling smarter—freeing teams to focus on higher-value work while delivering better client outcomes.

Employee satisfaction

Donnelley Financial Solutions (DFIN) is a leading global provider of compliance and regulatory software and services, helping organizations navigate some of the most complex moments in business. DFIN supports investment company regulatory compliance, complex capital markets transactions, and essential financial reporting across the corporate lifecycle.

In that environment, speed and precision matter.

“Our CEO says we have three constituents that we’re responsible for,” Katie Rice, SVP of Workplace Services and Infrastructure at DFIN, says. “Our stockholders, our clients—and our employees.”

At DFIN, employee service had to work as efficiently as the business itself.

A support model employees didn’t trust

Before Zendesk, DFIN outsourced its IT help desk. The experience created friction at every turn.

“If you called about your password reset, a rep would say, ‘I’ll log a ticket and have someone call you back,’” Rice says. “That’s an awful experience.”

Over time, employees stopped using the help desk altogether, turning instead to people they knew in IT.

“People were circumventing the help desk entirely,” says Rice. “They weren’t getting a good experience.”

What looked like low ticket volume was misleading—the demand was there, but it wasn’t being captured.

Rebuilding service in 90 days

DFIN decided to bring support back in-house and start over. The timeline was aggressive: 90 days to stand up a new service model, staff it, and launch globally.

The team needed a platform that could support phone and email immediately and scale fast.

“Zendesk didn’t say, ‘Absolutely not,’ when they heard our timeline,’” Rice says. “Instead, they asked, ‘How much time are you going to invest in making sure this is successful?’”

With support from Zendesk Professional Services and a focused internal effort, DFIN launched on day 90—prioritizing core functionality first, then expanding over time.

“As soon as people realized they were going to get a good experience, our call volume went up, our ticket volume went up—and everything went up,” says Rice.

The increase was not a problem—it was proof.

From friction to trust

Today, Zendesk powers DFIN’s global employee service experience across phone, chat, email, and self-service. Employees know they can reach someone who understands their work.

“They know they’re going to get a hold of someone who can actually solve their problem,” says Rice.

DFIN resolves the vast majority of issues quickly, with 80 percent of tickets handled in a single touch and 99 percent employee satisfaction.

“We’ve worked through about 90 percent of the friction employees used to experience,” Rice says. “My boss joked that he should put 100 as the employee satisfaction KPI because then I’d at least have to strive for something.”

Zendesk allows DFIN to support chat, email—and phone—so the company’s employees can use the channel they prefer.

“You can’t build a support platform based on how you would like it to work,” says Rice. “It needs to be centered on how people will actually use it.”

From visibility to optimization

When DFIN first brought support in-house, ticket volume surged. What appeared to be low demand before had simply been hidden by a system employees didn’t trust.

“As soon as people realized they were going to get a good experience, everything went up,” Rice says.

But over time, that trend reversed. With better data, faster resolutions, and continuous improvements to the employee experience, DFIN began reducing demand at the source.

“Since peaking in 2022, our company has seen a 42 percent reduction in overall tickets and a 59 percent reduction in calls,” Rice says.

That shift was driven by several factors, including improving first-time resolution rates, replacing outdated hardware more frequently, and using insights from Zendesk to address recurring issues before they escalated.

Turning service into insight

DFIN gained something with Zendesk it did not have before: clear, actionable data.

“Before Zendesk, our customer experience team would flag an issue—for example, we would get 25 tickets about problems with uploads,” Rice says. “And we would ask our product engineering team to work on that issue, but it was always a vague request—‘People are complaining about uploads, can you look into it?’”

Now, Rice and her team can identify root causes, track trends, and act on real signals.

“With Zendesk, we now have access to actual data,” Rice says. “We don’t have to rely on people’s gut feelings.”

A proving ground for customer experience

At DFIN, employee service is more than an internal function, it’s a testing ground for innovation. Workflows, channels, and AI capabilities are tested internally before reaching customers.

“Sandboxes help, but they cannot replicate real-world usage,” Rice says. “You need real people to actually use the product—and surface any issues.”

Employees provide immediate, candid feedback, helping the team refine experiences before they reach customers.

“Our employees are blunt. They’ll say, ‘I don’t know what you guys changed—but this sucks now,’” says Rice. “And that’s a very different conversation if a client were to say the same thing.”

Unifying customer and employee service operations

With a strong foundation in employee service, DFIN turned its attention to customer experience. The company brought in a chief client experience officer to assess the landscape. What she found was fragmentation.

“There were multiple product lines, and each one was operating as a separate entity,” Rice says. “One was using Salesforce. Another was using a homegrown platform. And the third was relying on an email inbox.”

Without consistency, performance was difficult to manage.

“With all the different platforms, she couldn’t get reporting,” Rice says. “She couldn’t get metrics. She had no visibility into any data.”

Looking for a path forward, she turned to DFIN’s CIO, who pointed her to Zendesk.

“I met with her and walked through how we were using it on the employee side,” says Rice. “One of the best things about the Zendesk platform is that it’s so easy to customize things.”

What stood out wasn’t just the platform—it was the speed.

“She had a very aggressive timeline,” Rice says. “But we knew that we could meet that deadline with Zendesk.”

DFIN brought multiple global teams onto a single platform, replacing disconnected systems with a unified, data-driven approach to customer service.

“And we were able to get the CX side up and running in under six months,” says Rice.

Built to fit a complex ecosystem

DFIN operates in a highly regulated environment, requiring multiple systems to work together seamlessly. Zendesk serves as the user-facing service layer, while ServiceNow supports backend ITSM processes.

Instead of forcing a single system to do everything, DFIN defined clear roles for each and connected them through automation.

“We create as many automations and alignments as we can so we’re not doing duplicate work,” says Rice.

Zendesk’s flexibility allows teams to adapt the service experience quickly without disrupting critical systems, while integrations streamline workflows across the stack.

“The ease of customizing what we need—and how quickly we can make changes—directly impacts how fast we’re able to move,” Rice says.

Scaling service with AI

Rice and her team are actively working to connect Zendesk’s Copilot with an existing Microsoft Copilot Studio agent, as part of a broader effort to scale service without increasing headcount.

“We’re looking at what we can build to take work off the team’s plate,” Rice says.

AI is already improving efficiency through automated summaries and better visibility into tickets, helping supervisors coach agents and respond faster.

Looking ahead, DFIN is focused on expanding automation and deflection, reducing the need for human intervention on repetitive requests.

In terms of getting her team on board, “Do you want to reset passwords all day?” Rice asks. “Or would you rather spend time with the person who keeps calling every week and figure out what their actual problem is?”

AI isn’t replacing people—it’s enabling them to deliver higher-value work, while helping the team manage demand more efficiently.
